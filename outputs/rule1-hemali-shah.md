## ONE AVATAR: The Waiting Woman

It is 6:30am. She has already made breakfast, answered three WhatsApp messages from family, and checked her work email. The mirror catches her for a second — she looks presentable, composed, capable. But behind the put-together face, something pulls. She used to know exactly who she was. She used to feel something when she laughed that wasn't performance. The mood swings come without warning now. The hot flashes arrive at the worst times. Her family looks at her with confusion or quiet frustration, not understanding what's happening. She doesn't fully understand it either. She types "women's retreat Dubai" into Google. Then closes the tab. "Maybe later," she tells herself. She has been saying maybe later for three years.

**The turning point trigger:**
A conversation — with a friend, a colleague, a woman she barely knows — who mentions she went on a retreat and "came back different." Something in that phrase lands differently than it usually would. She asks more questions than she normally would. She thinks about it for days. Then she finds She Travels.

**Her internal monologue:**
"I am so tired of pretending I'm fine. I look at women like Hemali and wonder how they seem so alive. Is it selfish to want something just for me? My family needs me. But I need something too — I just don't know exactly what it is anymore. I want to feel like myself again. I want to feel like there's still something that belongs just to me. Maybe if I had some time to actually breathe — really breathe — I could figure out who I am now."

## ONE PROBLEM: THE VILLAIN

The problem is not the woman. The villain is what has been happening to her.

**External:** She is exhausted, emotionally depleted, suffering through menopause without support or understanding, and running on empty while everyone else's needs are met before hers.

**Internal:** She feels invisible — to her family, to the world, and worst of all, to herself. She questions whether she deserves more. She has lost the thread back to who she truly is.

**Philosophical:** "A woman who has given everything she has to her family, her work, and her community should not have to beg for permission to exist for herself."

**The Villain Statement:**
"The real problem in The Waiting Woman's story is the invisible cultural demand that she give everything and need nothing — which shows up as exhaustion, emotional depletion, and menopause misunderstood by everyone around her — makes her feel undeserving and invisible to herself — and represents the unfair truth that the most giving women in the world are consistently the last to be given to."

## ONE SOLUTION: 3-STEP PLAN

**Step 1 — Pause (Arrival & Welcome):**
She arrives. A curated wellness welcome kit is waiting — candle, crystal bracelet, everything chosen with intention. For the first time in years, there is nothing she must do for anyone else. A grounding meditation. Conscious dining with nourishing food. Breathwork. Journaling. Stillness. Every element of day one says the same thing: this space is entirely for you. She exhales for the first time in months.

**Step 2 — Let Go & Reconnect (Days 2 and 3):**
Through sunrise meditation, gentle yoga, breathwork, fire ceremonies, candle release rituals, and meaningful exploration of a new place — she begins to release what has been holding her down. In the sharing circle, she hears other women's stories and recognises herself in them. She is not alone. She shares her own story — perhaps for the first time. The successful women she meets remind her of what is possible. The conversations about perception, about energy, about habits — she carries them home in her body, not just in her notes. Where focus goes, energy grows.

**Step 3 — Return Renewed (Day 4 — Farewell):**
She boards for home reset, realigned, and risen. She has new practices she actually wants to do. A sisterhood that doesn't end when the retreat does. And a new relationship with herself — one where she no longer waits for someone to give her permission to choose herself. She knows who she is again.
