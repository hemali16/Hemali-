## SECTION 3: THE CORE PROBLEM THEY WANT SOLVED

Extracted from Hemali's own words across her answers.

**Problem 1:** She is carrying old wounds and complaints — sometimes literally physical pain — that trace back years, even decades, and she keeps "reliving the trauma" instead of moving past it. As Hemali put it, describing a relative: she was "complaining about one of the injury what she had" from when she was pregnant — and her kids are now grown and of marriage age, but she's "feeling the same pain."

**Problem 2:** Existing wellness retreats only address one piece of her — the body, through massage and yoga — while leaving the rest of her (mind, energy, emotion, connection) untouched, and they're priced so high that "everybody cannot afford" it.

**Problem 3:** Group travel itself has become a performance — an "unsaid competition" about who looks better, who takes a better photo, who's posting the best version of the trip on Instagram — rather than genuine connection or exploration.

**Why they haven't solved it yet:**
She's tried the expensive, wellness-only retreat (good, but one-dimensional and unaffordable for most). She's tried traveling with family (but that means going for spas, not connection with strangers or new skills). She's tried group travel (but that became about appearances, not meaning). None of these combine mind, body, energy, emotion, and connection into one experience — which Hemali describes as the actual gap in the market.

## SECTION 4: WHAT THEY WANT INSTEAD

**Tangible outcome:** A retreat experience that blends practical wellness (movement, rest) with deeper work (journaling, being heard, releasing old grief) — not a checklist of activities, but a complete reset.

**Emotional outcome:** To feel heard. Hemali was specific about this: in her own words, "majority of the time women want to be heard," and her journaling exercise — writing every complaint, every unmet need on paper, then tearing it up — exists specifically so a woman can release what she's been carrying and finally feel "peace and joy."

**Identity outcome:** To stop being defined by an old story of pain and start moving forward — to become, in Hemali's words, someone who takes "action to come out of" what she's been carrying rather than narrating it for years.

## SECTION 7: ICP PERSONA SUMMARY

She is a woman in her 50s who has spent decades being everything everyone else needed — to a husband, to children, to a household — and somewhere in that, she stopped tracking who she actually is. She still carries pain from years ago, sometimes literally retelling the same injury or grief as if it happened yesterday, because she has never had a structured space to put it down. She has tried the expensive wellness retreat and felt good physically but not fully met. She has traveled with her family and her friends, but it became about appearances rather than connection. What she actually wants — though she may not have the words for it yet — is a few days where someone finally asks her how she's doing and means it, where her body, her mind, and her grief all get attention in the same space, and where she leaves not just rested but heard.
